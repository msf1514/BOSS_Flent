02 / 08The problem, sharpened
BOSS · Problem 1

This is a trust problem, not a pricing problem.

Flent carries the vacancy risk on every home it signs. The rate we anchor to is a median of nearby listings, and that median is quietly built on evidence that often is not true. The maths is easy. Knowing which rows to believe is the hard part.

~27,000
Bangalore listings behind the benchmark. Comparables are picked by society first, then by config and furnishing.
30 to 4
Once the junk is removed, an area that looked like 30 comparables can hold just four real ones.
~1 month
of breakeven pushed back by roughly ten extra vacant days. Anchoring to a wrong rate is what causes those days.
Who feels it

The Supply and Acquisition reviewer who signs a multi-year lease, and the P&L behind them. At a sample of four, one bad listing moves the estimate by thousands of rupees.

Why it is dangerous

The failure is invisible. The dashboard shows the same clean figure whether the sample was trustworthy or not, so a confident number built on bad evidence is worse than an honest blank.

The sharper framing: reduce the confidence, do not just clean the cells.02 / 08

04 / 08Shape of the system
BOSS · Problem 1

Everything is captured. Only listings are calculated.

The decision the whole system rests on: capture is not calculate. Photos, comments, outcomes and deal terms are all preserved with their source, but only the listing CSV is ever allowed to move the market median. That is how opinions never quietly become numbers.

Lane 1 · Market comparables
public, scraped · fails as duplicate, stale, bait
Portal adapters normalise to one 13-column contract
Trust layer: dedup, flag, score confidence
Feeds the market-rate median
Lane 2 · Deal terms
private, negotiated · fails as unconfirmed, verbal
Deal record and Slack, per-field evidence status
Confirmation layer: review before commit
Stays a labelled deal fact, never the median
Maintenance, capex and the landlord ask are absent from Lane 1 by construction, so a scraper can never invent them onto a listing. The two lanes meet only at the human decision layer.
Human judges
  • Overrules any row, with a reason
  • Owns and resolves evidence tasks
  • Signs the completion
AI assists
  • Reads messy inputs, extracts
  • Suggests a value and its source
  • Never commits it silently
Code does the maths
  • Median, dedup, confidence tiers
  • Same inputs give the same output
  • Every line is inspectable
An automation nobody can audit is just a faster way to be wrong.04 / 08

06 / 08When the evidence runs out
BOSS · Problem 1

Every number opens the listings behind it. And it can say no.

The required failure case

Feed a deliberately thin or contradictory pull and the tier returns INSUFFICIENT. The honest answer is “we cannot say yet”, not a manufactured number.

Missing is not zero

A blank stays blank and is shown as unknown. We never score a missing value as 0 to keep a formula running.

Disagreement is shown, not averaged

A reviewer can re-include or exclude any row, and conflicting calls stay visible in the audit trail.

The one hard no

Inventing evidence. No synthetic row poses as real. If it is not in the packet, it is a labelled assumption.

Confidence is derived from three checks: sample size, source diversity, leave-one-out stability.06 / 08
07 / 08Scope · assumptions · how we would know
BOSS · Problem 1

What we cut on purpose, and how we would know it works.

Deliberately not solved
  • The verdict and any blended deal score. That is Problem 3.
  • Achieved rent. Listings are asking prices, signed reality is Problem 2.
  • Photos, comments and outcomes inside the number. Captured, never calculated from.
  • Live scraping in the graded path. The seam is designed, a crawler is a drop-in later.
Assumptions, labelled
Comparability
Same BHK and furnishing, society match, area within a tolerance, seen within a max age.
Median
Positional median over mean, so one extreme listing cannot drag the rate.
Thresholds
Sensible, documented numbers, not Flent’s real internal cutoffs.
First metric to watch

Share of runs a reviewer accepts without override, a proxy for trust, tracked next to the median shift caused by overrides. If reviewers constantly overturn the engine, the rules are wrong, not the reviewers.

The trade-off we chose

A smaller, defensible sample over a larger, impressive-looking one. Four trustworthy comps beat thirty noisy ones, and where nothing qualifies, we say so.

How AI was used

AI scaffolded the modules and the UI, ran an adversarial code-review pass on each change, and pressure-tested the framing, always asking “is this capturing or calculating?”. Judgment stayed human on the boundaries.

Clear beats clever. Every claim traces to evidence, weak deals die early.07 / 08

Problem 3 · Decision page
  • Reads, does not recompute the frozen benchmark and terms.
  • Routes each comment to the domain that owns it, so a demand note never moves the market number.
  • Shows confirmed versus assumed, plus the flip conditions. A named human signs.
Problem 2 · Feedback loop
  • Joins acquisition-time predictions to day-90 actuals on the same registry.
  • Honours censoring: cancelled, still-filling and not-mature cases are shown but excluded. A blank is not a zero.
  • Reports systematic bias per micromarket, and where there is no clean ground truth.
Both previews use only real packet data and invent nothing. The prior assessment explains the model, it never feeds back as an engine input, or the system would reproduce its own answer.
