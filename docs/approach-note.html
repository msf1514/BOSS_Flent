02 / 08The problem, in our words
BOSS · Problem 1

It’s a trust problem, not a maths problem.

Flent carries the vacancy risk on every home it signs. The rate we anchor to comes from a median of nearby listings — and that median is quietly built on evidence that often isn’t true.

~27,000
Bangalore listings behind the benchmark — comparables chosen by society, then config & furnishing.
30 → 4
Once the junk is removed, an area that looked like 30 comparables can hold just four.
~1 month
of breakeven pushed back by ~10 extra vacant days — the cost of anchoring to a wrong rate.
Who feels it

The Supply / acquisition reviewer who signs a multi-year lease, and the P&L behind them. At a sample of four, one bad listing moves the estimate by thousands of rupees.

Why it’s dangerous

A confident-looking number built on bad evidence is worse than an honest blank. The failure is invisible: the dashboard shows the same clean figure whether the sample was trustworthy or not.

“Not a black box. Not a spreadsheet where opinions quietly become numbers.”— the brief
03 / 08Approach · and what we won’t solve
BOSS · Problem 1

Clean honestly, then say how much to trust it.

What we build
  • Staged, deterministic cleaning — every valid listing → matches this home → trusted, so you see what each stage removed and why.
  • A named reason on every row — duplicate, bait, aspirational, mislabel, stale — flagged for a human, never silently dropped.
  • An explicit confidence tier — HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / INSUFFICIENT, derived, not asserted.
  • Row-level override — include, exclude or defer any listing with a required reason; the prior engine state is recorded.
  • A frozen, versioned handoff — one immutable market-evidence packet for the wider deal.
What we deliberately don’t solve
  • The verdict. No ACQUIRE / NEGOTIATE / HOLD / PASS, no blended deal score — that’s Problem 3.
  • Achieved rent. Listings are asking prices; signed-lease reality is Problem 2. Achievable-rent confidence is hard-coded LOW.
  • Photos, comments, outcomes into the number. Captured and attributed — never entered into the median.
  • Live scraping in the graded path. The seam is designed; a real crawler is a drop-in later, so runs stay reproducible.
Going deep on one narrow slice beats going shallow on all of BOSS.

04 / 08Shape of the system
BOSS · Problem 1

Two lanes, one wall between capture and calculate.

Every input has an origin, and the origin dictates the pipeline. Public listings and private deal terms fail differently, so they’re handled differently — and they meet only where a named human decides.

Lane 1 · Market comparables
public · scraped · fails: dup / stale / bait
Portal adapters → one 13-column contract
Trust layer: dedup · flag · confidence
→ the market-rate median
Lane 2 · Deal terms
private · negotiated · fails: unconfirmed / verbal
deal.md / Slack → per-field evidence status
Confirmation layer: review-before-commit
→ a labelled deal fact, never the median
The two lanes meet only at the human decision layer — maintenance, capex and landlord ask are absent from Lane 1 by construction, so a scraper can never invent them onto a listing.
Human judges
  • Override any row, with a reason
  • Resolve evidence tasks
  • Signs the completion
AI assists
  • Reads messy inputs, extracts
  • Suggests structured values + source
  • Never commits silently
Code does maths
  • Median, dedup, tiers
  • Same inputs → same output
  • Every line inspectable

05 / 08Missing · stale · conflicting · unreliable
BOSS · Problem 1

Every kind of junk gets a name, not a shrug.

The brief’s five failure modes each become a specific, inspectable reason attached to the listing — routed to a human, never silently included or silently deleted.

Duplicate
Same flat re-posted across portals — kept once so it can’t inflate the sample.
Bait price
Priced far below market to harvest enquiries. Pulled from the rate.
Aspirational
Optimistic ask that rarely transacts — often the only number in the dataset.
Mislabel
Wrong BHK / furnishing / society, inconsistent across platforms.
Stale
Rented weeks ago, still live — measured against the evidence cutoff.
Missing ≠ zero

A blank stays blank and is shown as unknown. We never score a missing observation as 0 to keep a formula running.

Disagreement shown

A reviewer can re-include or exclude any row; conflicting calls stay visible in the audit. We don’t average disagreement away.

The one hard no

Inventing evidence. No synthetic row poses as real; no fabricated number fills a gap. If it isn’t in the packet, it’s a labelled assumption.

Thresholds are chosen, documented and testable — not production hurdles.05 / 08

07 / 08Assumptions · scope cuts · trade-offs
BOSS · Problem 1

Labelled calls, made on purpose.

Assumptions (labelled)
Comparability
Same BHK & furnishing, society-family match, built-up area within a set tolerance, seen within a max age of the evidence cutoff.
Median
Positional median over mean — one extreme listing can’t drag the rate.
Duplicate
Same config + area within tolerance + identical deposit → one representative (owner / freshest wins).
Thresholds
Sensible, documented numbers — not Flent’s real internal cutoffs.
Scope cuts & trade-offs
  • Problems 2 & 3 ship as an approach + a static, clearly-labelled preview — not code.
  • Comment / photo / outcome ingestion is captured by the evidence registry but calculated from nothing.
  • Lane 2 (deal terms) is a designed, stubbed contract — the shape is honest and complete, the workflow is Problem 3.
  • Trade-off: a smaller, defensible sample over a larger, impressive-looking one. Four trustworthy comps beat thirty noisy ones.
When in doubt, we prefer the honest, narrower answer over the impressive-looking one. That instinct is the whole submission.

08 / 08Validate first · the metric · AI use
BOSS · Problem 1

What we’d watch, and how we’d know it works.

First thing to validate

That the reviewer agrees with the engine’s keep/drop set on real pulls — the trust layer only earns its place if humans accept its calls more often than they overturn them.

First metric to watch

Share of runs accepted without override (a trust proxy), tracked beside the median shift caused by overrides. If reviewers constantly overturn the engine, the rules are wrong — not the reviewers.

Convincing evidence
  • Deterministic: identical inputs → an identical run (asserted in tests).
  • Every flag traces to a rule; no invented values.
  • A thin input reliably returns INSUFFICIENT.
  • Overrides are auditable and re-run the median.
How AI was used

AI scaffolded modules and the UI, ran an adversarial code-review pass on each change, and pressure-tested the framing — “is this capturing or calculating?”. Judgment stayed human on the boundaries: what enters the median, where to say INSUFFICIENT, and what to deliberately not build.
