02 / 10The problem, sharpened
BOSS · Problem 1

This is a trust problem, not a pricing problem.

Flent carries the vacancy risk on every home it signs. The rate we anchor to is a median of nearby listings, and that median is quietly built on evidence that often is not true. The maths is easy. Knowing which rows to believe is the hard part.

~27,000
Bangalore listings behind the benchmark. Comparables are picked by society first, then by config and furnishing.
30 to 4
Once the junk is removed, an area that looked like 30 comparables can hold just four real ones.
~1 month
of breakeven pushed back by roughly ten extra vacant days. Anchoring to a wrong rate is what causes those days.
Who feels it

The Supply and Acquisition reviewer who signs a multi-year lease, and the P&L behind them. At a sample of four, one bad listing moves the estimate by thousands of rupees.

Why it is dangerous

The failure is invisible. The dashboard shows the same clean figure whether the sample was trustworthy or not, so a confident number built on bad evidence is worse than an honest blank.

The sharper framing: reduce the confidence, do not just clean the cells.02 / 10

04 / 10System design · the one wall
BOSS · Problem 1

Everything is captured. Only listings are calculated.

The design decision the whole system rests on: capture is not calculate. Photos, comments, outcomes and deal terms are all preserved with their source, but only the listing CSV is ever allowed to move the market median. That is how opinions never quietly become numbers.

Lane 1 · Market comparables
public, scraped · fails as duplicate, stale, bait
Portal adapters normalise to one 13-column contract
Trust layer: dedup, flag, score confidence
Feeds the market-rate median
Lane 2 · Deal terms
private, negotiated · fails as unconfirmed, verbal
Deal record and Slack, per-field evidence status
Confirmation layer: review before commit
Stays a labelled deal fact, never the median
Maintenance, capex and the landlord ask are absent from Lane 1 by construction, so a scraper can never invent them onto a listing. The two lanes meet only at the human decision layer.
Human judges
  • Overrules any row, with a reason
  • Owns and resolves evidence tasks
  • Signs the completion
AI assists
  • Reads messy inputs, extracts
  • Suggests a value and its source
  • Never commits it silently
Code does the maths
  • Median, dedup, confidence tiers
  • Same inputs give the same output
  • Every line is inspectable
An automation nobody can audit is just a faster way to be wrong.04 / 10

BOSS · Problem 1

Five steps, so nobody lands on it confused.

1
Upload
Drop a listings CSV, or try it anonymously with your own pull.
2
Clean and score
The trust layer runs the funnel and returns a rate with a confidence tier.
3
Review row by row
Inspect every flagged listing and overrule any call with a recorded reason.
4
Assign the gaps
Raise evidence tasks to a named teammate. They block completion until resolved.
5
Freeze
Lock one versioned market-evidence packet for the wider deal.

Collaboration here is concrete, not a slogan. An evidence gap becomes a task with an accountable role, a named person, and a notification, and the review cannot be frozen until those tasks are resolved. Market Ops, Pricing, Property and Acquisition each own the items only they can answer, and the history records who did what.

The header tracker always shows where the review stands and the single next action, so a first-time user is never guessing what to do next.

06 / 10When the evidence runs out
BOSS · Problem 1

Every number opens the listings behind it. And it can say no.

The required failure case

Feed a deliberately thin or contradictory pull and the tier returns INSUFFICIENT. The honest answer is “we cannot say yet”, not a manufactured number.

Missing is not zero

A blank stays blank and is shown as unknown. We never score a missing value as 0 to keep a formula running.

Disagreement is shown, not averaged

A reviewer can re-include or exclude any row, and conflicting calls stay visible in the audit trail rather than cancelling out.

The one hard no

Inventing evidence. No synthetic row poses as real. If it is not in the packet, it is a labelled assumption.

Confidence is derived from three checks: sample size, source diversity, leave-one-out stability.06 / 10

We want you to point the trust layer at your own pull and watch it work. In anonymous mode the CSV is read and analysed entirely in your browser. It is never uploaded, it is never written to our database, and nothing about it is kept once you leave the page. We cannot leak what we never receive.

08 / 10Scope · assumptions · how we would know
BOSS · Problem 1

What we cut on purpose, and how we would know it works.

Deliberately not solved
  • The verdict and any blended deal score. That is Problem 3.
  • Achieved rent. Listings are asking prices, signed reality is Problem 2.
  • Photos, comments and outcomes inside the number. Captured, never calculated from.
  • Live scraping in the graded path. The seam is designed, a crawler is a drop-in later.
Assumptions, labelled
Comparability
Same BHK and furnishing, society match, area within a tolerance, seen within a max age.
Median
Positional median over mean, so one extreme listing cannot drag the rate.
Thresholds
Sensible, documented numbers, not Flent’s real internal cutoffs.
First metric to watch

Share of runs a reviewer accepts without override, a proxy for trust, tracked next to the median shift caused by overrides. If reviewers constantly overturn the engine, the rules are wrong, not the reviewers.

The trade-off we chose

A smaller, defensible sample over a larger, impressive-looking one. Four trustworthy comps beat thirty noisy ones, and where nothing qualifies, we say so.

How AI was used

AI scaffolded the modules and the UI, ran an adversarial code-review pass on each change, and pressure-tested the framing, always asking “is this capturing or calculating?”. Judgment stayed human on the boundaries.

Clear beats clever. Every claim traces to evidence, weak deals die early.08 / 10

BOSS · The wider system

A hand-off, then a loop. Problem 1 is the first link.

The frozen market-evidence packet is the object everything else consumes. The decision reads it, the outcome tests it, and the loop makes the next estimate better. Neither Problem 2 nor Problem 3 is allowed to recompute the rate.

The spine

One evidence registry with hash, source, actor and time. Outcomes and prior assessments are just more artifact kinds on the same spine.

Cross-team by design

Supply owns market evidence, Acquisition owns the call, Finance and Pricing own economics, Property owns condition. Each hand-off is explicit.

The circularity guard

The prior assessment and past outcomes are shown to explain, never fed back as an engine input, or the system would reproduce its own answer.

Freeze, then decide, then observe, then calibrate. Same evidence, one direction.09 / 10
10 / 10Problems 2 and 3 · approach
BOSS · How we would ship the rest

Two more pages, each built on the same evidence.

Problem 3 · The decision page

One view for the person signing, so they never open another spreadsheet. It reads the frozen benchmark and the deal terms and never recomputes them.

  • Each comment routed to the domain that owns it, so a demand note never moves the market number.
  • Confirmed versus assumed shown plainly, so a call on three guesses does not look like one on five facts.
  • Flip conditions: exactly what new evidence would change the answer, and which way.
  • A named human authorises the verdict. BOSS recommends, a person signs.

Built as a preview from the real packet at /decision.

Problem 2 · The feedback loop

Puts what we forecast next to what actually happened by day 90, so we can see where we run high and where we run low, per micromarket.

  • Outcomes are another registry artifact. We join acquisition-time predictions to day-90 actuals.
  • Censoring is honoured: cancelled, still-filling and not-mature cases are shown but excluded from error stats. A blank is not a zero.
  • We report where the estimate is systematically biased, and where there is no clean ground truth to score against.
  • Fill-time optimism is the costliest error, since vacant days come straight out of P&L.

Built as a preview on the packet’s 14 real cases at /calibration.

Both previews are illustrative and clearly labelled. They use only real packet data, invent nothing, and keep the capture-versus-calculate wall intact across every problem.
