02 / 08Before · the problem in our words
BOSS · Problem 1

It’s a trust problem, not a maths problem.

Flent carries the vacancy risk on every home it signs. The rate we anchor to comes from a median of nearby listings — and that median is quietly built on evidence that often isn’t true.

~27,000
Bangalore listings behind the benchmark — comparables chosen by society, then config & furnishing.
30 → 4
Once the junk is removed, an area that looked like 30 comparables can hold just four.
~1 month
of breakeven pushed back by ~10 extra vacant days — the cost of anchoring to a wrong rate.
Who feels it

The Supply / acquisition reviewer who signs a multi-year lease, and the P&L behind them. At a sample of four, one bad listing moves the estimate by thousands of rupees.

Why it’s dangerous

A confident-looking number built on bad evidence is worse than an honest blank. The failure is invisible — the dashboard shows the same clean figure whether the sample was trustworthy or not.

BOSS · Problem 1

Two lanes, one wall between capture and calculate.

Every input has an origin, and the origin dictates the pipeline. Public listings and private deal terms fail differently — so they’re handled differently, and they meet only where a named human decides.

Lane 1 · Market comparables
public · scraped · fails: dup / stale / bait
Portal adapters → one 13-column contract
Trust layer: dedup · flag · confidence
→ the market-rate median
Lane 2 · Deal terms
private · negotiated · fails: unconfirmed / verbal
deal.md / Slack → per-field evidence status
Confirmation layer: review-before-commit
→ a labelled deal fact, never the median
Maintenance, capex and landlord ask are absent from Lane 1 by construction — a scraper can never invent them onto a listing. The lanes meet only at the human decision layer.
Human judges
  • Override any row, with a reason
  • Owns & resolves evidence tasks
  • Signs the completion
AI assists
  • Reads messy inputs, extracts
  • Suggests values + a source
  • Never commits silently
Code does maths
  • Median, dedup, tiers
  • Same inputs → same output
  • Every line inspectable
An automation nobody can audit is just a faster way to be wrong.04 / 08
05 / 08Missing · stale · conflicting · unreliable
BOSS · Problem 1

Every kind of junk gets a name — and stays inspectable.

Duplicate
Same flat re-posted across portals — kept once so it can’t inflate the sample.
Bait price
Priced far below market to harvest enquiries. Pulled from the rate.
Aspirational
Optimistic ask that rarely transacts — often the only number in the data.
Mislabel
Wrong BHK / furnishing / society, inconsistent across platforms.
Stale
Rented weeks ago, still live — measured against the evidence cutoff.
Missing ≠ zero

A blank stays blank and is shown as unknown. We never score a missing observation as 0 to keep a formula running.

Disagreement shown, not averaged

A reviewer can re-include or exclude any row; conflicting calls stay visible in the audit trail.

The one hard no

Inventing evidence. No synthetic row poses as real; no fabricated number fills a gap. If it isn’t in the packet, it’s a labelled assumption.

Nothing is deleted — each flagged listing stays reviewable, and you can overrule any call.05 / 08

07 / 08The riskiest assumption, proven
BOSS · Proof of work

And when the evidence runs out, it says so.

The assumption the whole system rests on: that honest cleaning changes what you can trust, not just the decimals. Proven on the real packet — and paired with the failure case the brief asks for.

Proven — the win is trust, not the number
  • Cross-posts collapsed (CP-0071 / CP-0075) → one representative.
  • Bait, aspirational, mislabel & impossible-area each flagged with a reason.
  • Confidence derived from sample size, source diversity & leave-one-out stability.
  • Deterministic: identical inputs → an identical run, asserted in tests.
The required failure case

Feed a deliberately thin, contradictory pull and the tier returns INSUFFICIENT — the honest “we can’t say yet” instead of a manufactured number.

A confident-looking number on bad evidence is the exact failure we refuse to ship.
Raw CSV in → structured, reviewable market evidence out, in five minutes — with the reason behind every decision preserved.

08 / 08Scope · assumptions · what we’d watch
BOSS · Problem 1

What we cut on purpose, and how we’d know it works.

Deliberately not solved
  • The verdict (ACQUIRE / NEGOTIATE / HOLD / PASS) & any blended score — Problem 3.
  • Achieved rent — listings are asking prices; signed reality is Problem 2.
  • Photos, comments, outcomes into the number — captured, never calculated from.
  • Live scraping in the graded path — the seam is designed; a crawler is a drop-in later.
Assumptions (labelled)
Comparability
Same BHK & furnishing, society match, area within tolerance, seen within a max age.
Median
Positional median over mean — one extreme can’t drag the rate.
Thresholds
Sensible, documented numbers — not Flent’s real internal cutoffs.
First metric to watch

Share of runs a reviewer accepts without override (a trust proxy), tracked beside the median shift caused by overrides. If reviewers constantly overturn the engine, the rules are wrong — not the reviewers.

Trade-off we chose

A smaller, defensible sample over a larger, impressive-looking one. Four trustworthy comps beat thirty noisy ones — and where nothing qualifies, we say so.

How AI was used

AI scaffolded the modules and UI, ran an adversarial code-review pass on each change, and pressure-tested the framing — “is this capturing or calculating?”. Judgment stayed human on the boundaries.
